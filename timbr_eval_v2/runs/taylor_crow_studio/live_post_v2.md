Most studios pick one decade of a training career and build for it. Taylor Crow Studio built for the whole arc.

## The studio

At 2717 E Union St in Madrona, Taylor Crow Studio runs as a private facility, one room, real equipment, no big-box floor and no rotating cast of strangers on the rack next to you. Crow founded the studio in 2022 after a two-month pay dispute with a corporate customer service employer, sixteen dollars an hour, contested and never fully resolved. Dog-sitting covered the gap. That money became the studio's first equipment order. Crow trained in martial arts starting at age twelve, earned a Zumba certification at sixteen, and spent high school cycling through sports medicine, powerlifting, CrossFit, barre, and dance fitness before graduating from the University of Washington in 2021 with a degree in dance. NASM certification followed, along with a client roster that spans total beginners to competitive athletes.

## Private, by design

Madrona sits on Seattle's east side, a short run down to Lake Washington and Madrona Park, a residential neighborhood with none of the commercial density of Capitol Hill or South Lake Union. Taylor Crow Studio uses that quiet on purpose. Sessions run one-on-one or in small groups capped low enough that Crow and her contracted trainers can correct form in real time, not from across a crowded floor. The format trades the scale of a franchise gym for a room where programming gets rebuilt around one client instead of averaged across two hundred.

## Training built around the whole life cycle

Programming at Taylor Crow Studio runs by life stage, not by template. Bone density work anchors sessions for older clients, strength training positioned as the direct countermeasure to osteoporosis rather than a bonus. Prenatal clients build muscle mass ahead of delivery, a measurable factor in lowering complication risk; postpartum clients rebuild through a structured return that accounts for recovery timelines most franchise gyms never program for. Menopausal clients train specifically to hold muscle mass, bone density, and joint mobility through a transition that also gets addressed for its effect on mood and energy, not just physical output. Four life stages, four different programs, one studio, and one coaching philosophy built on anatomy and kinesiology instead of a fixed template pulled off a shelf.

## Aerial conditioning and in-home beginners

Past the main floor, Taylor Crow Studio runs aerial conditioning, a specialty most Seattle gyms skip entirely. The work targets the shoulder stability, core strength, and flexibility that silks and rings actually demand, built from Crow's own background as a performer and choreographer rather than a certification picked up secondhand. Beginner-focused in-home training rounds out the range on the opposite end, sessions delivered outside a gym setting for clients who want a first rep taken somewhere lower-pressure than a studio floor. Two specialties, two very different entry points, one programming philosophy behind both.

## Corporate wellness

Corporate wellness runs as a third track, built for teams rather than individuals. Group city walks combine low-impact movement with real Seattle incline and enough pace to hold a conversation, a lower barrier to entry than a typical office fitness challenge. Group fitness classes get built for mixed ability levels inside one team, no single fitness bar every participant has to clear before showing up. Mobility and recovery sessions target posture and soreness over leaderboard metrics, one yoga mat per person, nothing else required. Inquiries get a response inside 48 hours, and programming gets built around a specific team's goals and access needs rather than a fixed package sold the same way twice.

## The team, and how they get paid

Six trainers run the floor: Crow, Carena Holmes, Juan Pablo "JP" Perez, a Doctor of Physical Therapy candidate, Meredith Trombly, Pallis Aurora, and yoga instructor Sophie Haegele. Contracted trainers work on a 90 percent commission split, a structural choice tied to Crow's stated position on fair pay for female, queer, and minority fitness professionals across the industry. The number sits well above the standard studio-trainer split, and it functions as the same instinct that started the studio in the first place, now built into how the studio runs its own payroll rather than left as a mission statement on a website.

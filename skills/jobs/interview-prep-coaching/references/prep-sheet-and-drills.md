# Prep Sheet Structure & Stress-Test Drills

## Prep sheet — proven layout
Lead with a tight read, not a wall. Structure that worked (Allen Institute, Jun 2026):

1. **The role, plainly** — one or two sentences, what the job actually is day-to-day.
2. **Header line** — company, role, date/time, interviewer name+title, platform.
3. **Strongest bridges** — 3 of Tanzim's resume items that map directly to the JD.
   Tie each to a phrase from the posting ("monthly analytics on KPIs" → his Power BI work).
4. **The landmines** — the 2–4 places the JD and resume DON'T line up. This is the
   highest-value section. For each: name the gap, name how to cover it.
5. **Their values to mirror** — pull the company's own language (e.g. Allen Institute:
   "Open Science, Big Science, Team Science") so he can echo it.

## Resume-vs-JD gap analysis — the method
- Read the resume and the live posting side by side.
- **System/tool gaps are the #1 landmine.** If the JD names a specific tool repeatedly
  (Allen Institute named **ServiceNow** 5×) and the resume doesn't have it, the interviewer
  WILL probe. Coach: name the gap once, don't bluff, pivot to a transferable proxy
  (JIRA/Asana for ServiceNow) + a fast-learning proof point (US Bank: picked up banking
  cold, top-10 district in 9 months). Never say "I'm a fast learner" — show it.
- **Overqualification.** When the role is a level below his CV (coordinator role, HS-diploma
  requirement, lower band), the interviewer asks "why this role + are you a flight risk."
  Coach a non-defensive reframe: operations/coordination is the *through-line* of his career;
  he chose THEM for the mission; loyalty proof via numbers (24hr Fitness 255th→87th, TIMBR
  retained across all 3 phases). Don't over-explain salary.
- **Domain gap** (e.g. facilities ops) — find the closest bridge in his history
  (Guckenheimer multi-site facility ops) and have him lead with it.

## Stress-test drills — how Tanzim wants them run
- **One question at a time.** Ask, wait for his answer, push back, THEN next question.
- Open with the hardest landmine question — the one they'll lead with.
- After his answer:
  - Grade honestly. Name what's vague, what tool names he dropped, what "why it matters"
    beat he skipped.
  - Give the tightened version in his own cadence (short, tool names landed, ends on
    commitment not a breezy "same thing different system").
  - **Make him say it back.** Don't accept reading the model answer — he repeats in his words.
- If he asks "how should I answer?" or "propose a full answer" → give the full first-person
  script, ready to deliver, then still have him repeat it.
- **Catch self-sabotaging phrasing in the script.** Tanzim has good instincts here — he'll
  sometimes flag it himself ("isn't it risky to mention the flight-risk section?"). He's right.
  Don't name the interviewer's doubt out loud ("flight risk", "overqualified") — naming it
  plants it. Fold the rebuttal in as positive commitment instead ("when I commit to a place I
  dig in and build" + loyalty numbers), and only meet the concern head-on if the interviewer
  raises it first. Dodging it once it's been named looks worse than addressing it.
- Transcription noise: his spoken-practice replies come through speech-to-text and mangle
  proper nouns ("Gia Sana"→JIRA/Asana, "Ella tracking"→SLA tracking, "Production service"→
  ServiceNow). Fix the term, remind him to enunciate it on the day, don't grade him down for it.

## Reference questions to drill (transferable across coordinator/ops interviews)
1. "Walk me through your experience with <the JD's named system>." (the tool-gap landmine)
2. "This role is a step below your background — why this job, and how do I know you won't leave?"
   (the overqualified / flight-risk double-barrel)
3. "Tell me about the team / why we joined / biggest challenge for this role" — flip to HIS
   prepared questions (the recruiter confirmation email often lists suggested questions to ask).

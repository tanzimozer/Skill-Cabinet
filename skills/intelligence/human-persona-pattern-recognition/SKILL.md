# Human Persona Pattern Recognition

## Purpose

Extract underlying behavioral patterns from surface-level signals to:
1. Identify the person's archetype/type
2. Predict preferences across domains they haven't explicitly stated
3. Increase relevancy of recommendations, content, and communication style
4. Anticipate needs before they're expressed

## Core Principle

**Surface preferences are symptoms of deeper identity structures.**

A person who likes jazz isn't just selecting music — they're expressing values around complexity, improvisation, sophistication, and comfort with ambiguity. These same values manifest across every domain of their life.

---

## The Five Layers of Persona

### Layer 1: Observable Signals
What they explicitly state or demonstrate:
- Music, films, books, podcasts
- Brands they use, clothes they wear
- How they spend free time
- Language patterns, vocabulary, emoji use
- Response timing and communication style

### Layer 2: Values Extraction
What the signals reveal about core values:
- **Complexity vs. Simplicity** — Do they seek layered experiences or clean/minimal?
- **Novelty vs. Tradition** — Early adopter or proven paths?
- **Control vs. Flow** — Structured planning or spontaneous?
- **Individual vs. Collective** — Stand out or belong?
- **Depth vs. Breadth** — Master one thing or sample everything?
- **Process vs. Outcome** — Journey-focused or destination-focused?

### Layer 3: Archetype Identification
Map values to behavioral archetypes (see Archetype Matrix below)

### Layer 4: Cross-Domain Prediction
Apply archetype patterns to predict unstated preferences

### Layer 5: Communication Calibration
Adjust tone, pacing, detail level, and framing to match their type

## Archetype Matrix

### The Optimizer
**Signals:** Tracks metrics, uses productivity apps, minimalist aesthetic, protein-focused diet, listens to podcasts at 2x
**Values:** Efficiency, measurable progress, control, outcome-focus
**Predicts:**
- Food: Functional (meal prep, macros, Soylent-adjacent), values nutrition data
- Travel: Optimized itineraries, flight hacks, lounge access
- Products: Best-in-class after research, reads reviews obsessively
- Music: Background/focus music, lo-fi, or nothing (music as tool)
- Communication: Bullet points, skip the preamble, give the answer

### The Curator
**Signals:** Niche interests, vinyl/film cameras, independent coffee shops, "you probably haven't heard of" energy
**Values:** Taste, authenticity, discovery, being ahead of mainstream
**Predicts:**
- Food: Independent restaurants, cuisine authenticity matters, hates chains
- Travel: Avoid tourist traps, local neighborhoods, "hidden gems"
- Products: Small brands, story matters, anti-Amazon
- Music: Deep cuts, album-oriented, genre-fluid but specific
- Communication: Reference-rich, appreciates wit, hates generic

### The Sensualist
**Signals:** Describes experiences vividly, foodie tendencies, texture/aesthetic-focused, "vibes" matter
**Values:** Pleasure, beauty, present-moment experience, richness
**Predicts:**
- Food: Flavor-first, willing to splurge, presentation matters
- Travel: Luxury touches, views, spas, no rushed itineraries
- Products: How it feels > specs, design-forward, tactile
- Music: Mood-based, emotional resonance, full albums
- Communication: Descriptive, paint pictures, don't rush

### The Builder
**Signals:** Side projects, "I made this," DIY tendencies, tools over services
**Values:** Creation, agency, learning by doing, ownership
**Predicts:**
- Food: Cooks at home, recipes as projects, equipment matters
- Travel: Active trips, learning something new, workshops
- Products: Repairable, customizable, raw materials
- Music: Often background while building, or lyrics about craft
- Communication: Show the how, respect their capability, skip hand-holding

### The Connector
**Signals:** Group activities, "we should all," shares constantly, knows everyone
**Values:** Relationships, belonging, shared experience, social proof
**Predicts:**
- Food: Shareable, group dining, the place everyone's talking about
- Travel: Who's coming matters more than where, trips with friends
- Products: What others use, recommendations over research
- Music: Playlists for parties, singalongs, shared cultural moments
- Communication: Inclusive language, "what do you think," group context

### The Seeker
**Signals:** Philosophy/spirituality adjacent, asks "why," journaling, therapy-positive
**Values:** Meaning, growth, self-understanding, depth
**Predicts:**
- Food: Intentional eating, connection to source, ritual around meals
- Travel: Transformative potential, retreats, nature immersion
- Products: Aligned with values, minimal but meaningful
- Music: Lyrics matter, emotional/spiritual resonance, concept albums
- Communication: Go deeper, welcome tangents, space for reflection

### The Performer
**Signals:** Aesthetic-conscious, social presence matters, curated online persona
**Values:** Recognition, impact, aesthetics, audience awareness
**Predicts:**
- Food: Photogenic, trendy spots, the story of the meal
- Travel: Postable destinations, unique backdrops, experiences > relaxation
- Products: Visible brands, aesthetic coherence, what it signals
- Music: Cultural currency, concert-going, artist associations
- Communication: Acknowledge their wins, audience-aware framing

### The Guardian
**Signals:** Routines, risk-averse language, "what we've always done," loyalty to brands
**Values:** Stability, reliability, tradition, proven paths
**Predicts:**
- Food: Comfort food, regular spots, suspicious of trends
- Travel: Return to favorites, family-oriented, low-friction
- Products: Established brands, warranties matter, skeptical of new
- Music: Catalog of favorites, nostalgia, resists new artists
- Communication: Reassurance, precedent, don't push too hard

## Application Protocol

### Step 1: Signal Collection
Gather observable data points without interrogation:
- What they mention liking/disliking
- How they describe things (adjectives reveal values)
- What they ask about
- Timing and style of communication
- What they share vs. keep private

### Step 2: Values Extraction
For each signal, ask: "What does choosing this over alternatives reveal?"
- Not just WHAT but WHY this specifically
- Look for patterns across 3+ signals before concluding

### Step 3: Archetype Mapping
- Primary archetype (dominant pattern)
- Secondary archetype (situational or emerging)
- Note contradictions — they're data, not errors

### Step 4: Hypothesis Generation
Generate predictions for unstated domains:
- "Given X, they likely prefer Y because Z"
- Confidence levels: high (3+ confirming signals), medium (2), low (1)

### Step 5: Validation Strategy
- Introduce predictions subtly, observe response
- Confirming data strengthens pattern
- Contradicting data refines model
- Never assume — always update

### Step 6: Application
- Recommendations: Match predicted preferences
- Communication: Calibrate to their style
- Anticipation: Surface needs before asked
- Framing: Position information in their value language

---

## Red Flags / Anti-Patterns

1. **Over-indexing on single signal** — One data point ≠ pattern
2. **Ignoring contradictions** — Humans are complex; contradictions are information
3. **Static typing** — People change; archetypes shift by context
4. **Projection** — Don't map your values onto their signals
5. **Stereotyping** — Archetypes are starting points, not boxes
6. **Ignoring stated preferences** — When explicit and implicit conflict, explicit usually wins

## Example Application

**Input signals:**
- Listens to Japanese city pop and 70s soul
- Takes photos on film camera
- Mentioned preferring "the small coffee shop on the corner, not Starbucks"
- Uses complete sentences in texts, rarely uses emoji
- Works in design

**Analysis:**
- Primary archetype: **Curator** (niche taste, anti-mainstream, aesthetic-conscious)
- Secondary: **Sensualist** (texture-aware, experience-focused via film/music choices)
- Values: Authenticity, craftsmanship, aesthetic intentionality, discovered-not-given

**Predictions (high confidence):**
- Food: Independent restaurants, probably Japanese or vintage-vibe spots, presentation matters
- Travel: Japan likely on list, boutique hotels over chains, photography-oriented itineraries
- Products: Vintage/secondhand positive, brand story matters, anti-algorithm
- Communication: Appreciate specificity, avoid generic recs, reference-rich works

**Calibration:**
- Don't recommend "top 10" anything
- Lead with "you might not have seen this but..."
- Specificity signals respect for their taste
- Skip the enthusiasm — understated confidence matches their frequency

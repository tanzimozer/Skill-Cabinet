# Tanzim Correspondence — Drafting Approach

## Core Principles

**No padding. No throat-clearing.** Open with substance. Tanzim's register is:
- Direct, technical, outcome-focused
- Respects the reader's time
- Buries resource constraints in framing, never states them nakedly
- Avoids hedging (I think, maybe, hopefully, we'll try)
- No thank you for reaching out opener unless genuinely needed

**Length is a feature.** Tanzim will correct both directions:
- **Too dense:** Walls of text without paragraph breaks, ideas bleeding together
- **Too short:** One-liners that omit necessary technical detail or context
- **Goldilocks:** 3–5 short paragraphs, each with a single idea, separated by whitespace

The right length is the minimum needed to establish credibility and technical depth without losing the reader to density.

## Workflow

### Step 1: Understand the Context
- Who's the recipient? (investor, advisor, peer, potential customer?)
- What's the ask or response to? (pitch, correction, new opportunity, feedback loop?)
- What does Tanzim want the reader to do or believe after reading?
- Are there resource constraints or scope limits that need burying?

### Step 2: Structure First
Typical shape for a substantive response:
1. **Acknowledge** (1 sentence): Show you understood their point.
2. **Your position** (2–3 sentences): State the direction or approach plainly.
3. **Technical depth** (2–4 sentences): Explain how or what you're building — specific enough to establish credibility.
4. **Timeline or next step** (1 sentence): Forward momentum.

Skip flowery intros. Lead with the thing.

### Step 3: Draft in Short Paragraphs
- One idea per paragraph.
- Separate paragraphs with a blank line (visual breathing room).
- No paragraph longer than 3–4 sentences.
- Avoid nested clauses — break them into separate sentences.

### Step 4: Bury Resource Constraints
If you're limited on budget, time, or people, frame it as prioritization or phasing, not limitation:
- ✅ "We're starting with [A], then layering in [B] as the model matures."
- ✅ "The roadmap is 2–3 years, with [phase 1] in year one."
- ❌ "We don't have the resources for X right now."
- ❌ "We can only afford to do Y."

The reader should infer constraint from smart sequencing, not from you saying it.

### Step 5: Remove Hedge Language
- ✅ "PT comes first to the marketplace."
- ❌ "We think PT might come first..."
- ✅ "This modernizes the pipeline."
- ❌ "This could potentially help modernize..."

Tanzim doesn't sound uncertain. If you're unsure, don't write it; ask him.

### Step 6: Iterate on Density
When Tanzim says:
- **Too long** → Cut 25–30%. Remove secondary examples, condense explanations, kill the second example of the same point.
- **Too short** → Add technical specificity. Why does this matter? What's the mechanism? What's different about your approach?
- **Too heavy on the eye** → Same word count, but break into shorter paragraphs. Add whitespace. One idea per paragraph, max 2 sentences per idea.

## Pitfalls

- ✗ Opening with gratitude or social niceties when responding to a technical question. Get to the point.
- ✗ Explaining why something is hard unless asked. Readers care about the solution, not your obstacles.
- ✗ Using we when you mean I or vice versa. Be clear on whose voice this is.
- ✗ Leaving vague language in a draft thinking Tanzim will clarify it. Do that work yourself.
- ✗ Writing a draft longer than 6 paragraphs without checking if it can be compressed.
- ✗ Using unfortunately, admittedly, interestingly — signal words that weaken the statement.

## Example Flow (Session June 12, 2026)

**Context:** Maureen sent Tanzim an email about PT + fitness integration gaps. Tanzim drafted a response, asked for more professional, less vague. First draft was ~200 words, structured but dense.

**Feedback:** Too long → Second draft was 2 sentences → Too short → Final draft: 4 short paragraphs, ~140 words, hit the balance.

**Pattern:** The iteration showed Tanzim's preference is for white space and short paragraphs, not raw word count. A dense 140 words reads differently than a spacious 140 words.

## Register Examples

**Investor/advisor correspondence:**
```
Dear X,

[Acknowledge their insight or question — 1 sentence]

[Your approach — 2–3 sentences, technical specificity]

[Why this matters — 1–2 sentences]

[Timeline or next step — 1 sentence]

Best,
Tanzim
```

**Peer/partner update:**
```
[Straight into substance — no hope you're well]

[What you're doing]

[Why it works]

[What's next]
```

**Resource-constrained project pitch:**
Phrase as: We're launching with [core feature], then [expansion] in phase two as demand/metrics warrant. Never say we can't afford X.

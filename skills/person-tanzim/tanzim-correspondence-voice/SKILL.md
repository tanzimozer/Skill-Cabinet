# Tanzim Correspondence & Voice

## The core rule (learned the hard way, Jun 2026)

**When Tanzim gives you his words for an email, KEEP HIS WORDS.** Your job is mechanical cleanup, not authorship. He writes in clipped, fragment-heavy notes (`Stability: extremely risky and I'm aware. Hanging by a thread, but taking the risk because of opportunity cost.`) — that terse, colon-led, fragmentary style **is his voice on purpose.** Do not smooth it into flowing full-sentence prose.

**What happened:** He dictated content for a reply to Maureen. I rewrote it into elegant, expanded paragraphs ("A few corrections and clarifications, in the order you raised them..."). He said: *"you changed it, i want my version back."* Lesson burned in.

## What "edit this email" actually means for Tanzim

Default scope — do ONLY this unless he asks for more:
1. **Fix spacing and obvious typos** (missing spaces after punctuation, `'m` → `I'm`, doubled words).
2. **Light punctuation** — em-dashes where he clearly meant a break, capitalisation of sentence starts.
3. **Preserve his structure verbatim** — his `Topic: fragment.` format, his line breaks, his word choices, his ordering.
4. **Keep his fragments as fragments.** Don't promote them to full sentences.

Do NOT, unless explicitly asked:
- Reword for "flow" or "polish"
- Add framing sentences, transitions, or a structured preamble
- Expand his shorthand into elaborated prose
- Change his tone (he is direct and economical; don't make him sound formal or effusive)

## Iterative editing protocol

He edits **segment by segment**, pasting a revised chunk and expecting you to slot it in with minimal touch. When he pastes a replacement segment:
- Drop it in close to verbatim — fix only spacing/typos in THAT segment
- Re-print the **full current draft** each time so he can see the whole thing
- Don't re-polish segments he already approved
- Hold any open/trailing line (e.g. he trailed off at "my youngest") and **flag it once** rather than inventing an ending

## Things to still do (the value-add that's welcome)

- **Flag factual errors, once, plainly.** E.g. he attributed two essays to Dario Amodei; only *Machines of Loving Grace* is verifiably his. Say so and let him decide — don't silently "correct" by deleting.
- **Flag odd phrasings** but offer to restore his exact words. E.g. "desk magnets" → likely "desk-bound"; I substituted and told him, offering his version back. That's the right move.
- **Note where a line is unfinished** ("Still trailing at 'my youngest' — finish that, or send as is?").
- **Offer to send vs. let him copy across** — ask which, don't assume.

## Honest-correction style (he values intellectual honesty)

When he asks for something you can't verify (a link, an attribution, a fact), say "I can't attribute that reliably" rather than inventing it. He explicitly prizes "I don't know" over a confident guess. This applies to hyperlinks especially — only link URLs you can stand behind; flag the rest for him to source.

## Platform note

iCloud Mail and most plain email clients do NOT render markdown. When he wants a hyperlink, also give the **bare URL** so he can paste it, since `[text](url)` will show as literal text.
